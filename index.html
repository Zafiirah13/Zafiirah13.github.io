My journey

I grew up in Mauritius, a small island in the Indian Ocean, where I fell for physics long before I knew what machine learning was. After a BSc in Physics at the University of Mauritius, I won SKA and North-West University bursaries for an MSc in Astrophysics & Space Science in South Africa, where I was the first to use a GAN to augment radio-astronomy images.

A Newton Fund DARA Big Data scholarship (one of only three) took me to Jodrell Bank, University of Manchester, for my PhD. The pipelines I built there run on the MeerKAT and MeerLICHT telescopes, and my thesis won the Institute of Physics Computational Physics Thesis Prize.

Then I moved from stars to people: climate risk at a FinTech, then Microsoft Research Cambridge (face tracking, synthetic data, Gaussian splats and avatars for Microsoft Mesh), and now VLM evaluation in the Teams AI Lab.

AI that's measured, not assumed

My day job

A vision-language model can describe a meeting fluently and still miss who is speaking, invent an object, or fall apart when the camera is far away. My job is to measure what it gets right, where it fails, and why.

1

Define "good"

A clear task and rubric for each meeting scenario.

2

Test on real scenarios

Representative video: rooms, lighting, camera distance, number of people.

3

Score what matters

Perception, grounding, reasoning over time, and hallucinations.

4

Slice the results

Break down by occlusion, head pose, resolution and frame rate to find real failure modes.

5

Calibrate and track

Check automatic judges against people, then track everything on dashboards.

Sample slice report

Same model, same questions, different conditions. An overall score of 0.82 hides a much weaker tail.

Finding: heavy occlusion and low resolution drive most errors. Fix the data and capture conditions before changing the model.

What the numbers taught me

1

Risk-adjusted beats raw

Volatility targeting cut returns from 23% to 13% but lifted Sharpe from 0.58 to 0.85.

2

Baselines are brutal

Equal-weight long-only (0.79) beat long-short momentum (0.07).

3

Check behaviour, not only PnL

My deep momentum net learned a small, steady long bias: drift, not timing.

4

Respect time and regimes

Walk-forward validation, lagged signals, and the COVID-19 crash exposed an overfit model.
